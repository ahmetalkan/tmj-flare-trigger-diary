A jaw-pain flare diary: food, stress, posture and clenching

Temporomandibular disorders often come and go. A fortnight of dated notes will not cure a flare, but it can show a clinician whether meals, desk posture, daytime clenching or a rough week line up with the pain. This diary is that record — not a treatment plan.

The National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research’s TMD page is the US federal starting point for these disorders. MedlinePlus’s TMD overview, and its medical-encyclopedia article on the same condition, treat jaw-joint and muscle pain as a medical issue to be assessed rather than as a single “TMJ” product. Use those pages for definitions; use the diary below for what happened in your own weeks.

Four triggers worth separating

A guide to TMJ flare-up causes is the page to read first so you are not treating every bad day as the same problem. Next to it, the articles on TMJ and stress and on TMJ and posture split two causes that often get mixed together: a tense week, and a head-forward desk setup. Clenching can sit in both columns. On each diary row, mark the ones that were actually present that day instead of circling all of them out of habit.

  1. Fill the row the same evening, not from memory a week later.
  2. If a food or a long drive lines up with a jump in the afternoon score, circle it once and see whether it repeats — one event is an anecdote, three is a pattern worth mentioning.
  3. Take the sheet to a dentist or TMD clinician rather than starting devices or exercises from a video. TMJ Specialist Guide lists US clinics and is a reasonable place to start the search after you have the diary in hand.
A flare diary is not a diagnosis. New swelling, a locked jaw, fever, or pain after an injury needs prompt clinical care rather than another week of note-taking.
